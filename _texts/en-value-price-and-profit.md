### Historical Context

In June 1865, Karl Marx delivered this address in two sessions to the General Council of the International Workingmen's Association (First International) in London. It was delivered in English to counter the views of citizen John Weston, who argued that wage increases were futile because capitalists would simply raise prices to compensate.

### Key Theoretical Insights

- **Value and Price**: Prices fluctuate around the socially necessary labour-time embodied in commodities, rather than being determined arbitrarily by capitalist will.
- **The Rate of Surplus Value**: A rise in wages does not automatically produce inflation; rather, it directly compresses capitalist profit margins, making day-to-day economic struggles over wages and working hours essential fronts of the class struggle.
- **The Ultimate Goal**: While defending defensive strike actions for higher wages, Marx concludes with the famous revolutionary admonition: *Instead of the conservative motto: 'A fair day's wage for a fair day's work!' working class militants ought to inscribe on their banner the revolutionary watchword: 'Abolition of the wage system!'*
