### Historical Significance

Written in the fateful months between the July Days and the October Insurrection of 1917, *The State and Revolution* reasserts the uncompromising revolutionary essence of Marx and Engels against the pacifist and social-democratic distortions of Karl Kautsky and Eduard Bernstein.

### Foundational Theses

- **The State as an Organ of Class Rule**: The state is the product and manifestation of the irreconcilability of class antagonisms. It consists of special bodies of armed men, prisons, and coercive institutions.
- **Smashing the Bourgeois State**: The proletarian revolution cannot simply take possession of the ready-made state machinery and wield it for its own purposes; it must smash, shatter, and dismantle the capitalist standing army and bureaucracy.
- **The Dictatorship of the Proletariat and Withering Away**: The transitional commune-state (*semi-state*) suppresses the resistance of the exploiters, progressively withering away as classes dissolve into a communist society of associated producers.
