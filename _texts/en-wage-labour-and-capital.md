### Historical Overview

*Wage Labour and Capital* represents one of the clearest and most accessible introductory expositions of Karl Marx's economic theory. Based on lectures delivered to the German Workingmen's Club of Brussels in late 1847, the text was published in the *Neue Rheinische Zeitung* in April 1849 and later edited with an introduction by Friedrich Engels in 1891.

### Core Theoretical Contributions

- **Labour-Power as a Commodity**: Marx demonstrates that what the worker sells to the capitalist is not their 'labour' directly, but their *capacity to work* (labour-power) for a definite duration in exchange for the means of subsistence.
- **The Genesis of Profit**: The exchange value of labour-power (wages) is less than the value newly created by its expenditure during the working day, generating surplus value (*Mehrwert*) appropriated by capital.
- **Capital as a Social Relation**: Capital is not simply accumulated physical instruments or material goods, but a specific bourgeois social relation of production founded upon the separation of the producers from the conditions of labour.
