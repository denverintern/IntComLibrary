### Theoretical Context

Published in *Rassegna Comunista* in April 1921 following the founding of the Communist Party of Italy (PCd'I) at Livorno, *Party and Class* represents the definitive formulation of the Communist Left on the relationship between class consciousness, economic struggles, and revolutionary political organization.

### Key Concepts

- **The Class as a Dynamic Concept**: A class is not a statistical or sociological headcount of individuals engaged in manual labour. The proletariat only becomes a class for itself when it is unified by an autonomous historical programme and a centralized political party.
- **Refutation of Workerism and Syndicalism**: The party does not emanate mechanically from factory committees or trade unions, which are inevitably bounded by immediate economic competition and corporatism. The revolutionary party unites the advanced minority of the class on the basis of internationalist communist doctrine.
